How long will my case take?

What happened to every case filed in India's district courts in 2010–2018: 80.9 million cases, 620 districts. Pick a district and a type of case.

How to read this, and its limits
  • Typical time is the median: half of such cases were decided sooner, half later. "Decided" means any final disposal (judgment, dismissal, withdrawal, settlement, transfer), not necessarily a judgment in someone's favour.
  • Cases still pending when the data was collected (late 2018 to 2020, depending on the state) are counted as undecided up to that date, using survival analysis, so slow cases are not left out.
  • This is the past, not a prediction for one case. Courts have changed since 2018 (COVID, e-filing, new judges), and a single case depends on its facts, lawyers and adjournments.
  • Case types are grouped from the case-type names each court uses, which vary; "Other / unclassified" collects what could not be grouped.
  • Only district and subordinate courts are covered, not High Courts or the Supreme Court. Districts with fewer than 200 cases of a type are not shown.
Data: Development Data Lab, eCourts case records 2010–2018 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Method and code: CourtSight (time_to_decision.py).
