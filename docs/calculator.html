How to read this, and its limits
  • Typical time is the median: half of such cases were decided sooner. "Decided" means any final disposal (judgment, dismissal, withdrawal, settlement or transfer), not necessarily a judgment in someone's favour.
  • Cases still pending when the data was collected (late 2018 to 2020, depending on the state) are counted as undecided up to that date, so slow cases are not left out. Where the curve stops, there is no more follow-up.
  • This describes the past. It is not a prediction for one particular case: courts have changed since 2018, and each case depends on its own facts and adjournments.
  • Case types are grouped from the names each court uses; "Other / unclassified" collects what could not be grouped. Districts with fewer than 200 cases of a type are not shown. High Courts and the Supreme Court are not covered.
  • The eCourts records hold about 61% of the cases the official figures say were filed in 2015–2018, from 8% in Tripura to nearly all in Bihar; these figures describe the courts that were recorded. Districts whose records hold almost no decisions (such as Gaya) are withheld rather than shown wrong. See the audit.
