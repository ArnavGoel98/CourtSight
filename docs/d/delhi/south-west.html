How long do court cases take in South West?

Half of the civil suit cases filed here in 2010–2018 were decided within 22 months (all of India: 2.5 years). 38% were still pending three years after filing (India: 44%), which ranks South West 219 of 577 (1 = fewest pending; 90% range 207–231).

Judges here stayed a median of 1 months in one courtroom (all districts: 12), and about 5 courtrooms were active in an average month of 2014–2017. Across districts, longer postings go with faster civil cases, but this is a correlation, not a cause (details).

Of the cases pending here when the data was collected, 0% were next listed for summons, notices or appearance (India: 37%), and the next hearing was set a typical 69 days after the last one (India: 56). Across districts, longer gaps go with slower courts, especially for criminal cases; that is a correlation, not a cause. More

Read this with care

  • Records here are complete only from 2015. Cases filed earlier are counted from then, a correction for the uneven arrival of eCourts.
  • For Delhi, the case records used here hold about 41% of the cases the official figures say were filed in 2015–18. Figures here describe the courts these records cover.
  • "Decided" means any final disposal: judgment, dismissal, withdrawal, settlement or transfer. A slow district is not necessarily a badly run one: case mix, vacancies and local practice differ.
